## The two regressions on `main` right now

Both in `decorator.py`, both from SPEC-033 (merged in #125), both reproduced:

- **A stop signal stolen during shutdown's drain.** `_offer_orphan_signal` skips on
  `_live_worker()`, which returns `None` the instant `_shutdown_done` latches — so for the whole
  drain an orphan log replaces the sink's `stop_signal` with a fresh **unset** event, the one the
  drain thread is about to wait on. Measured: `shutdown(timeout=3)` expired with the sink left
  open, against a 0.30 s control. SPEC-035 FR-001.
- **A swap racing a shutdown leaves its sink owned by nobody.** `config.sink is B` true, `B`
  never closed, `health()` entirely clean. SPEC-035 FR-003.

## The harness that keeps the audit honest

`tests/test_promises.py` — each stated promise × each entry path (orphan, traced, async,
post-shutdown). **8 cells are `xfail(strict=True)` naming their audit finding.** A spec that fixes
one makes the cell XPASS, which *fails the build*, forcing the marker's removal. That is the
mechanism stopping the audit decaying into prose. SPEC-036 and SPEC-037 each clear cells.

## Process changes made this session, and why

In `docs/process.md`. Both came from the SPEC-033 post-mortem — eight review rounds, two defects
shipped anyway.

- **Rotate the frame, don't add rounds.** Cap same-frame review at two; exit when a *new* frame
  finds nothing; at least one pass must start from the library rather than the diff; require a
  reproduction rather than an argument.
- **Re-audit the rule, not the line.** Three reviewers told SPEC-033 "ownership, not liveness",
  each naming a different call site; each was fixed; a fourth shipped. SPEC-035 FR-002 is that
  lesson made executable — an AST-derived enumeration of every call site with its category.

## Things I would tell my successor

- **Verify a mutation applied before believing what it tells you.** A `str.replace` that matches
  nothing is a silent no-op, and a green result then reads as "the test is vacuous". This bit me
  once this session.
- **Time and counters are usually the wrong observable.** Three of my tests passed against the bug
  they claimed to catch because they measured elapsed time, or a gauge that had already unwound,
  or a call that took an unlocked fast path and acquired nothing. The thread a close ran on, and a
  count against the sink, are the observables that hold.
- **Register an `atexit` probe first**, or LIFO makes it run before the library's handler and
  report stale state. I got a measurement wrong twice this way.
- **Scope added mid-review restarts the clock.** Two of SPEC-033's regressions were in scope added
  during its own review — arriving late, scrutinised least, inheriting confidence it had not
  earned.
