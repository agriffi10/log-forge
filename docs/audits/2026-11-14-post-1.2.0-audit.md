The first audit of this project after a release rather than before one: run the day `v1.2.0` was
tagged, on Python 3.13.1 on macOS, from a detached worktree, by five fresh-context read-only
agents with one question each. No driver extra was installed, so every SDK sink was probed
through a stand-in client, and the lifecycle frame ran no repository test: "proven" in its matrix
means a test of that name was read.

**Outcome: the standing suite does not find this repository's defects; reviews and probes of the
neighbouring change do, one at a time.** Of the twelve lifecycle and sink fixes from `95945c0f` to
`717d0c68`, eight credit a review or hand-over of an adjacent change and none credits an existing
test, gate or CI failure; each then added a test written from its own reproduction, 9,015 test lines
in all, and none to the one generative test (verification frame, §4 and §5). A choke point quieted
its class wherever one was made: the delivery engine, for the sinks on it, and the closed-guard
helper in the 1.2.0 redesign (history frame, *Pattern*), and the diagnostics module earlier, under
SPEC-029 (the same frame's class 11). The lifecycle was given a single state holder four times and
no choke point: its decisions still span several lock sections with sink code running between them,
and ten of the eleven fixes after the arc added state or a predicate while none removed any
(lifecycle frame, detail D). The 23 findings below are what five frames found in one day by running
things, and three of them are about why nothing found them sooner.

## 3. Corrections made while bringing it in

Each is a place where the synthesis, or the frame behind it, said something its evidence does not
support. The frame reports are untouched.

1. **Finding 4 is not SPEC-076's doing.** The lifecycle frame says SPEC-076's closed guard made
   §12's "must not become a loss" false, and the synthesis repeats it. `p4b` run unchanged
   against the trees at `v1.0.0`, `v1.1.0` and `3835a30e` (the parent of SPEC-076's commit
   `3d65cf32`; each extracted with `git archive <ref> src`) loses the same events, 1 of 4 and 2
   of 8 lines; only the abandon line differs, four emit attempts on those trees and one at
   `a0f44b5f`, where a closed sink's refusal is final. `v1.0.0` already carries SPEC-045, and a
   `FileSink` refused after `close()` there, so the pin held only for a sink that keeps
   accepting: the pin's test double, `BufferingSink` in `tests/test_owed_closes.py`, never
   refuses. The frame also probed one direction only, the incoming sink having been a child of
   the outgoing graph. The direction §12 leads with, `configure(A)` then
   `configure(MultiSink(A, B))`, is `lifecycle/p4b_forward.py`, which is `p4b` with its two
   `configure` lines exchanged and was added when the audit was brought in. On all four trees
   the file again held 1 of 4 and 2 of 8, the rest counted in `health().sink.failed` (3 and 6)
   and announced as a skipped `MultiSink` child.
2. **Finding 6 is not a loss.** The synthesis ranks it **L**. In `p6` every event the child wrote
   reached a sink; the defect is which one. It is **S** here, and the row keeps the measured
   output so that a reader can disagree with the letter.
3. **Finding 19 is not a hang.** The synthesis ranks it **H**. In `p7` the child's `shutdown()`
   returns; what is wrong is the state it leaves and the span it did not deliver. It is **S**,
   with the measured output in the row for the same reason.
4. **`l` did not mean what rows 8 and 9 show.** The synthesis defines it as counted loss. A kill
   mid-handler leaves no counter and no stderr (`p12`), and at an expired exit "no counter
   survives the process" (`p04`). The legend now says each row states whether it is counted, and
   **H** says that finding 20 is a bounded self-wait.
5. **Finding 8** said events stay on the span "until it closes". The core frame's detail C and
   `README.md:1490-1492` show a `flush()` from inside the traced code, on the same thread or
   task, sweeps them.
6. **Finding 12** named Kafka and Pub/Sub alike. The frame reproduced Kafka and read Pub/Sub.
7. **Finding 5** said "with no count". That holds when one reply is lost; when every reply is
   lost the events are counted as lost (`failed=10`, `flush()` falsy) while each landed 16
   times.
8. **Finding 17** said a span that fails to open loses "its events". It loses its two boundary
   events; an in-span log is delivered as an orphan on a fresh trace (`p11`, reading note).
9. **Finding 10's home was not "none".** *A frozen surface is keyword-first, and says what it
   will not grow* keeps the worker's tunables unreachable from `configure()`, and invariant 10
   repeats it. Neither the frame nor the synthesis mentions the decision.
10. **The arc has five tracks, not four.** The synthesis says four and lists five.

One number moved without anyone being wrong: the sinks frame gives finding 1's loss as between 2
and 14 of 50 over four runs, and later runs lost 15, 25 and 21.

## 5. Settled decisions that bound a finding

- **Finding 7.** A dead drain thread is reported, not restarted (`docs/decisions/failure-paths/`).
  P8's 30 stranded later spans follow from that and are not a finding; the held batch being in no
  counter is.
- **Finding 2.** The settled record is divided on it, and the §12 item names each side with its
  path. A truthy `flush()` over a counted loss rests on `architecture.md` §13's first constraint
  ("SPEC-026's routing working as designed"), on *An event a sink cannot encode costs only itself,
  and a batch of nothing encodable settles, counted*, which is Andrew's of 2026-11-04 and shipped in
  the `v1.2.0` release notes, and on invariant 7's recorded exceptions. It contradicts *`flush()`
  reports delivery, and answers from the drain that carried the events*, whose entry says "`True`
  means the sink took them", and with it `results.py:61` and the README's "evidence of delivery". So
  neither closure is the default: each reverses one side and has its costs, which the item weighs.
  Finding 12 is the same case for Kafka.
- **Finding 5.** The duplication is the accepted cost of *A client exception costs its chunk, and is
  provable non-delivery for it* (`docs/decisions/sink-delivery/`), whose entry records that a
  re-send after a lost reply may duplicate, "taken on expected cost". That decision was written for
  R2 of the 2026-09-02 audit, and it is the decision, not either earlier audit, that records the
  lost-reply cost; for `HTTPSink` the resend is the predicate rule of *A sink declares what it
  retries, and anything else fails at once*. Sixteen sends is the 1.x total retry budget, `(sink
  max_retries + 1) × (worker max_retries + 1) − 1` retries (Andrew, 2026-11-04;
  `docs/specs/ARCS.md`, *1.2.0 stability redesign*). None of that is open. What is new is only that
  the resend is uncounted and carries no idempotency key.
- **Finding 10.** The worker's tunables stay unreachable from `configure()`
  (`docs/decisions/public-api/`). Nothing decided on 2026-11-14 reopens it; the §12 item says so
  and leaves the choice to Andrew.
- **Finding 14.** A keyed reply is read by what `Failed` names (*A positional response
  adjudicates all of a chunk or none of it*, `docs/decisions/sink-delivery/`). Real AWS names
  every entry; the open part is a reply that is not reply-shaped at all.
- **Finding 18.** `dropped` counting submissions is a recorded exception of invariant 2, and it
  stands. The ledger Andrew took appends event-unit terms to `Health`; the existing counters keep
  their units and nothing is reopened.

## 6. What was decided, and by whom

Andrew, in chat on 2026-11-14, after the findings were reported: "I'll take your recommendations
other than the mac os specific one", "Always keep main updated", and "You can additionally
evaluate and do any of the outstanding spec work as a part of this".

- **Taken:** an adversarial model test; one event-unit ledger; one sink conformance corpus keyed
  on acknowledgement class; the two Draft specs built; lifecycle commands serialized through one
  owner in place of a fifth round of case fixes, prototyped against the adversarial harness
  before it is committed to; one stable stop signal per sink, with the change to the public
  `log_foundry_stop_signal` protocol stated to him, which §12 holds open until a spec says
  whether 1.x sinks keep working or invariant 10 takes a recorded exception; a CI hang timeout
  with a stack dump and a nightly soak of the model test.
- **Declined:** a macOS CI leg, recorded as its own decision in `docs/decisions/working-rules/`.
- **Not decided.** `flush()` judged on the ledger's loss total (finding 2) was in the recommendation
  he accepted, but by the orchestrating session's account he was not told that the settled record
  is divided on what a truthy `flush()` means (§5), so it waits on him, with neither option the
  default: the truthiness changes to reflect loss, or it stays and `FlushResult` gains counts of
  what was lost in the window. Shipping an open span's events before the span closes (finding 8) was
  put to Andrew as his call, with no recommendation attached, and was unanswered when this was
  written; it is the orchestrating session's proposed direction. Whether capacity becomes a setting
  (finding 10) is his too, and was not asked. Coverage measurement, a job timeout, `ResourceWarning`
  as an error and a property-based sink-graph generator are the orchestrating session's plan for the
  CI track: that coverage is unmeasured was stated to him as an observation, not recommended.
- **Made by the orchestrating session under that authority, to be confirmed in each spec's
  review:** a send whose reply was lost gains a count of the sends that may have duplicated, and
  an idempotency key wherever the destination can honour one. That changes neither settled rule.
  An `UNKNOWN` outcome or an exception escaping `deliver` is never resent (*No shipped sink
  declares itself idempotent*). What stays resendable, at the accepted cost, is a client
  exception the sink's retry predicate accepts, caught inside `deliver` and answered `RETRYABLE`.

## 7. Planned remediation

The plan as it stood on 2026-11-14, not an assignment: arc `audit-2026-11-14`
(`docs/specs/ARCS.md`), with spec numbers given as each spec is authored, one at a time. Five
tracks. A track groups a concern and names the files most of its work is in; it does not
partition the source, the tracks share files (`ARCS.md` names them), and whether two specs build
at once is decided per pair by the test in `docs/process/several-agents.md`.

- **Harness (tests only):** the adversarial model (finding 21), then a derived matrix of every
  callback against every public call, with a fork and a signal handler as calls.
- **Pipeline (`worker.py`, `decorator.py`, `api.py`, `results.py`):** the event ledger and the
  accounting defects (7, 16, 17, 18); the README idioms and a statement of the losses nothing
  can count; the exit drain (9); and three things that wait on Andrew: what a truthy `flush()`
  means (2, 12), capacity (10), and open-span events shipped on the worker's interval (8).
- **Sinks (`sinks/`):** acknowledgement classes and the conformance corpus (22); unacknowledged
  writes (1, 11, 12, 14 and RabbitMQ confirms); the count and the idempotency key for resends
  (5), and autocommit (15). SPEC-082 and then SPEC-084 (13) are built alongside the arc, not in
  it: neither carries the arc in its frontmatter.
- **Lifecycle (`_lifecycle.py`, `config.py`, `_fork.py`):** the release rule for a sink still
  reachable from the installed graph (4), first, because it is a live loss and independent;
  then, after the harness track, one lifecycle command path (3, 20, and the residual windows the
  sink-lifecycle decisions record); one stable stop signal per sink; a forked child rebuilt from
  its configuration (6, 19).
- **CI (workflows, `pyproject.toml`):** the hang timeout with a stack dump and the nightly soak
  Andrew took, and by the orchestrating session's plan a job timeout, `ResourceWarning` as an
  error, branch coverage and a property-based sink-graph generator (23).

After the tracks, a residue pass over what §12 still lists. Three things the frames raised and
did not verify are filed there too, each marked unverified with what verifying it takes: the
"Owed until the PR's CI is green" lines still unstruck in the delivery docs of SPEC-067,
SPEC-069, SPEC-070 and SPEC-080; `SQSSink.MAX_BYTES` against current AWS documentation, which the
sinks frame raised from memory; and the timing comments at `ci.yml:131` and in `pyproject.toml`,
against the 141 to 143 s the frame read from one CI run.
