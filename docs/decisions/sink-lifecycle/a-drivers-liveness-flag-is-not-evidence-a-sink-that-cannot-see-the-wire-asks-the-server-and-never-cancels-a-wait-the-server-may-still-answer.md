---
type: Decision
title: "A driver's liveness flag is not evidence: a sink that cannot see the wire asks the server, and never cancels a wait the server may still answer"
description: "`is_connected` flips only when the driver's own tasks notice, and those tasks need loop time between calls; a sink that reports delivery on the flag reports it for events that never left the process, and a PONG wait the sink cancels kills the driver's reader when the answer arrives late. (SPEC-061)"
---

**A driver's liveness flag is not evidence: a sink that cannot see the wire asks the server, and never cancels a wait the server may still answer** — `NATSSink` drove its private loop only inside `run_until_complete`, so `nats-py`'s reader, ping and reconnect tasks received CPU only while a call was in progress. Measured at `71e42dd` under `docker pause`, 75 of 75 one-event emits were accepted over 148 s with `is_connected` true throughout and nothing raised or counted (M1); after `docker stop` and `docker start` the sink was still refusing 40 s after the restart, its reconnect task pending forever (M2). The loop now runs on one daemon thread the sink owns from construction to `close()`, and for a client the sink connected itself, `emit` and `flush` send a PING through the driver's public `flush()` and proceed only when the PONG arrives inside `publish_timeout`; a core batch is confirmed by a second round trip after its last publish, or counted and announced (SPEC-061 FR-002, FR-003). **The round trip is never cancelled by the sink.** The driver's `flush(timeout)` cancels its PONG future on expiry, and a late PONG then reaches `_process_pong`, which calls `set_result` on the cancelled future: the `InvalidStateError` ends the reader task while the status stays CONNECTED, after which every `flush()` returns normally against a dead server — a false confirmation (M3). So the probe is one task waited with `asyncio.wait` under the caller's deadline and left running when it expires; at most one is outstanding, a pending one is reused by the next caller, a finished one is discarded because a PONG that arrived between calls says nothing about now, and one is discarded while pending only when the driver's reconnect counter shows its PONG queue was reset. A probe is answered only if it completed without raising, the client still reports itself connected, and the counter has not moved, because `flush()` returns without a PONG while the driver is reconnecting (M9). A latch refuses every later caller at once while the outstanding probe has gone unanswered for `publish_timeout`, read before the lock, so a stall costs one caller one budget rather than every caller its own; a confirmation whose buffer did not drain leaves such a probe behind for it to age, and that PING, sent over an undrained buffer, is liveness evidence only — a batch is confirmed only by a fresh PING sent after `pending_data_size` read zero, since one sent earlier may have overtaken the publishes. A second latch holds the same line for a publish the driver never returned from: it is abandoned at the deadline, counted wherever no confirmation will judge it (JetStream, an injected client), and dated at the instant it is abandoned. ~~Every later call, on every client, is refused before and after the lock until it finishes~~ — **refined by SPEC-104: it ages as the PING latch does.** A call, on every client, made within one `publish_timeout` of the abandonment waits for that publish on the loop until the window ends (`_await_stuck`, the first step of a round and of `flush()`), with `asyncio.wait` and never cancelling it, and goes on with what remains of its own deadline if the publish finished; a call that finds it still pending when the window ends, or that comes after the window, is refused `RETRYABLE` with the latch's error, before the lock and again under it, since it would only queue behind the same blocked force-flush. Nothing publishes while an abandoned publish is pending: the re-read after the wait is the guard whose failure would be silent, because SPEC-102's resend of an unattempted tail relies on that order and nothing else would show it broken. The wait is work, as the ack wait it continues is, so it reads no stop signal and a round of the exit drain waits too; it ends inside the call's own deadline, so the exit drain's bound stands. A stall a little longer than one budget then loses no batch, and a longer one costs its callers one window in all: the first caller queued behind the abandoning round passes the check under the lock while the publish is young and takes about `2 * publish_timeout`, and every caller behind it is refused under the lock. Three such waits are the application's thread, invariant 4's recorded exception: a level call on the orphan path; the step of `log_foundry.flush()` that runs the live sink's own `flush()` on the caller's thread after the drain, whichever way the drain went (`_flush_live_sink`), which `flush`'s timeout does not bound, so a `flush(timeout=T)` in the window overruns T by up to one `publish_timeout`; and `configure(sink=…)`, which waits on the drain thread, whose round now waits too, and can spend its whole `DEFAULT_SWAP_TIMEOUT`. The first two take about `2 * publish_timeout` when they queue on the lock behind the abandoning round. With an injected JetStream double, at `1dc174fb` and on SPEC-104's tree: an orphan-only process at the default 10 s budget took 0.0 s and 9.951 s in `flush(timeout=0.1)`; with a worker at a 1 s budget, 0.102 s and 0.952 s; `configure(sink=…)` at a 10 s budget, 0.414 s and 5.001 s; and an orphan level call queued behind the abandoning round at a 1 s budget, 0.899 s and 1.902 s (`docs/audits/2026-11-14-probes/core-pipeline/spec104_nats_flush_overrun.py`, `spec104_nats_swap_cost.py` and `spec104_nats_queued_orphan.py`). Invariant 3's bound, a timeout plus the sink's one in-flight call, still holds. **Both latches and every driver wait follow one timing rule, stated once on `DRIVER_DEADLINE_MARGIN`:** a timeout the sink hands the driver for a wait whose expiry the driver raises ends strictly inside the sink's own wait on it, because two timers due at once are decided by arming order and the sink arms first — handed the sink's bare remaining budget, every ack wait the budget capped read as an abandoned publish instead of a raised one; and every latch ages from the start of the call whose budget the stall spent, not from when the sink noticed it — a probe dated when the drained wait failed made the next caller wait a whole budget more, and one dated when that wait began still made it wait whatever the probe and the publishes had taken first. **One exception, since SPEC-104: the abandoned-publish latch ages from the abandonment.** An abandonment happens at its call's deadline, so dated from that call's start the latch would be one window old the instant it is armed and would refuse the very next caller at once, which is what lost a whole batch to a stall a little longer than one budget: the worker's rounds of a batch are 0.01 to 0.04 s apart, and at the stop they are not apart at all. A test holds the first half on the source; `tests/test_sinks_nats_matrix.py`, gated by mutation, holds both. `close()` does not call the driver's `drain()`, which swallows a cancellation and runs its `finally` flush to the driver's own 10 s whatever the caller's timeout — 12.00 s under a 2 s `wait_for` once a JetStream client holds a subscription (M7); it drives its own round trip and `client.close()` under one deadline, and a caller never waits on the loop without a limit: a bound the coroutine already enforces plus a slack, after which the loop is treated as wedged and abandoned rather than recovered. **Rejected for the abandoned-publish latch (SPEC-104)**, each prototyped on `docs/audits/2026-11-14-probes/core-pipeline/spec104_nats_exit_latch.py` (`transient`: one publish takes 3 s against a 2 s budget while every other acks in 1 ms, 2,000 events, `shutdown(timeout=10)`; at `61b942c0`, four runs a cell, the latch refusing at once lost `batch_lost` 1,632 to 1,636 at exit and 182 to 202 in the steady drain, and the chosen design 0 in both): keeping the engine's backoff on the exit drain and ending it at the deadline, which recovered only 1,339 of 2,000 events, changes every sink and reverses SPEC-027's rule that a shutdown cuts every backoff short; waiting in every round with no age bound, which closed both transient cases but let a long stall cost `flush(timeout=5)` its whole 5.01 s and `shutdown(timeout=10)` 9.04 s, since every round of the drain spent a budget and those calls wait on the drain thread, where the chosen design measured `flush` at 1.93 to 2.08 s and `shutdown` at 0.00 s under a 30 s stall; an exit-only wait keyed on a new `RunContext.stopped` engine flag, which leaves the steady drain's loss and adds engine surface; and a worker-only engine flag with the age bound, which measured the same as the chosen design but needs an engine change and treats the worker's calls differently from every other caller, where the PING latch treats them alike. **Rejected:** the driver's flag alone (M1, the defect); `Client.rtt()`, which has M3's cancel-on-timeout; a probe through `_send_ping` or `_pongs`, private names the next driver release may move; a per-call cancel-and-retry, which is M3 once per call; and `wait_for(client.drain(), D)`, which M7 shows cannot bound it. **Do NOT** cancel a PONG wait the server may still answer; do NOT stack a second PING behind an unanswered one; do NOT probe, confirm or drain an injected `client=`, which is bound to a loop the sink cannot drive (M10: a flush from a second loop returned only at its own timeout, 3.001 s in three of three); do NOT read `_pongs` or call `_send_ping`; do NOT let a PING sent before the pending buffer read zero confirm a batch; do NOT hand the driver a timeout equal to the sink's own remaining wait; do NOT date a latch from when the sink noticed the silence, and date every latch but one from the start of the call whose budget the stall spent — the abandoned-publish latch is dated from the abandonment, because that is its call's deadline and a start-of-call date would make it one window old the moment it is armed (SPEC-104); do NOT cancel an abandoned publish a later call waits on, and do NOT publish while one is pending, young or old. (SPEC-061, SPEC-104, arch §12, §13)

## History

### 2026-11-18

- **Corrected (SPEC-104 diff review, frame 2).** The refinement named only the orphan level call
  as a wait on the application's thread; `log_foundry.flush()`'s sink step and `configure(sink=…)`
  wait there too, and a call queued behind the abandoning round waits about two windows. Both are
  now stated in the entry, with the four measurements behind them.
- **Refined (SPEC-104).** The abandoned-publish latch now ages: it is dated at the abandonment, a
  call made within one `publish_timeout` of it waits for the publish on the loop until the window
  ends, never cancelling it and never publishing past it, and only a call after the window, or one
  whose wait ended with the publish still pending, is refused. The timing rule's dating half gains
  this one exception and the fence says why. Four alternatives were prototyped and rejected, with
  their numbers above. `spec104_nats_exit_latch.py` at `61b942c0`: `transient exit` went from
  `batch_lost` 1,632 to 1,636 to 0, and `transient steady` from 182 to 202 to 0; on SPEC-104's
  final tree, FR-003's library-level test of the exit case read `batch_lost` 0 and `sink_lost` 1.

### 2026-11-04

- **Refined (SPEC-061 diff review, sibling sweep).** The timing rule above, after three more instances of one class — a sink deadline racing a driver deadline, or a latch aging from the wrong moment — and the matrix corpus that holds it. The left-behind probe's anchor then moved once more, from when its drained wait began to the start of the call whose budget it spent: only that anchor makes the latch hold for the very next caller whatever the call spent before the stall, and the earlier one flaked a test one parallel run in four.
- **Refined (SPEC-061 diff review, system frame).** The latch now also ages a probe a confirmation leaves behind when the buffer does not drain, with the rule that such a PING never confirms; and a second latch, on an abandoned publish, covers the twin the probe cannot see — an injected client, whose abandoned core publish had been counted nowhere.

### 2026-11-03

- **Settled.**
