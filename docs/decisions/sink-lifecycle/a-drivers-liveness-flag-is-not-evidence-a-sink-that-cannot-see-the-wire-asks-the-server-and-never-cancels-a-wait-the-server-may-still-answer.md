type: Decision
title: "A driver's liveness flag is not evidence: a sink that cannot see the wire asks the server, and never cancels a wait the server may still answer"
description: "`is_connected` flips only when the driver's own tasks notice, and those tasks need loop time between calls; a sink that reports delivery on the flag reports it for events that never left the process, and a PONG wait the sink cancels kills the driver's reader when the answer arrives late. (SPEC-061)"
---

**A driver's liveness flag is not evidence: a sink that cannot see the wire asks the server, and never cancels a wait the server may still answer** — `NATSSink` drove its private loop only inside `run_until_complete`, so `nats-py`'s reader, ping and reconnect tasks received CPU only while a call was in progress. Measured at `71e42dd` under `docker pause`, 75 of 75 one-event emits were accepted over 148 s with `is_connected` true throughout and nothing raised or counted (M1); after `docker stop` and `docker start` the sink was still refusing 40 s after the restart, its reconnect task pending forever (M2). The loop now runs on one daemon thread the sink owns from construction to `close()`, and for a client the sink connected itself, `emit` and `flush` send a PING through the driver's public `flush()` and proceed only when the PONG arrives inside `publish_timeout`; a core batch is confirmed by a second round trip after its last publish, or counted and announced (SPEC-061 FR-002, FR-003). **The round trip is never cancelled by the sink.** The driver's `flush(timeout)` cancels its PONG future on expiry, and a late PONG then reaches `_process_pong`, which calls `set_result` on the cancelled future: the `InvalidStateError` ends the reader task while the status stays CONNECTED, after which every `flush()` returns normally against a dead server — a false confirmation (M3). So the probe is one task waited with `asyncio.wait` under the caller's deadline and left running when it expires; at most one is outstanding, a pending one is reused by the next caller, a finished one is discarded because a PONG that arrived between calls says nothing about now, and one is discarded while pending only when the driver's reconnect counter shows its PONG queue was reset. A probe is answered only if it completed without raising, the client still reports itself connected, and the counter has not moved, because `flush()` returns without a PONG while the driver is reconnecting (M9). A latch refuses every later caller at once while the outstanding probe has gone unanswered for `publish_timeout`, read before the lock, so a stall costs one caller one budget rather than every caller its own; a confirmation whose buffer did not drain leaves such a probe behind for it to age, and that PING, sent over an undrained buffer, is liveness evidence only — a batch is confirmed only by a fresh PING sent after `pending_data_size` read zero, since one sent earlier may have overtaken the publishes. A second latch holds the same line for a publish the driver never returned from: it is abandoned at the deadline, counted wherever no confirmation will judge it (JetStream, an injected client), and every later call, on every client, is refused before and after the lock until it finishes, since each would only queue behind the same blocked force-flush. `close()` does not call the driver's `drain()`, which swallows a cancellation and runs its `finally` flush to the driver's own 10 s whatever the caller's timeout — 12.00 s under a 2 s `wait_for` once a JetStream client holds a subscription (M7); it drives its own round trip and `client.close()` under one deadline, and a caller never waits on the loop without a limit: a bound the coroutine already enforces plus a slack, after which the loop is treated as wedged and abandoned rather than recovered. **Rejected:** the driver's flag alone (M1, the defect); `Client.rtt()`, which has M3's cancel-on-timeout; a probe through `_send_ping` or `_pongs`, private names the next driver release may move; a per-call cancel-and-retry, which is M3 once per call; and `wait_for(client.drain(), D)`, which M7 shows cannot bound it. **Do NOT** cancel a PONG wait the server may still answer; do NOT stack a second PING behind an unanswered one; do NOT probe, confirm or drain an injected `client=`, which is bound to a loop the sink cannot drive (M10: a flush from a second loop returned only at its own timeout, 3.001 s in three of three); do NOT read `_pongs` or call `_send_ping`; do NOT let a PING sent before the pending buffer read zero confirm a batch. (SPEC-061, arch §12, §13)
