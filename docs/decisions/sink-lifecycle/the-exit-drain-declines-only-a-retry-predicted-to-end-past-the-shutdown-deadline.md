---
type: Decision
title: "The exit drain declines a retry, or a later batch, predicted to end past the shutdown deadline"
description: "the first attempt of the exit drain's first batch is always made; a retry is declined when the batch's longest attempt plus a 0.1 s join margin is predicted to end past the `shutdown()` deadline, and a later exit batch is declined unattempted once an exit batch has declined a retry or the longest attempt any exit batch has made predicts it would overrun; once an exit batch is delivered, each later one is held to what that batch's rate fits in the time left less the margin, when the prediction would let it start; each declined batch is counted, and the budget is the caller's `timeout`, so there is no knob. (SPEC-062, SPEC-090, SPEC-105)"
---

**The exit drain declines a retry, or a later batch, predicted to end past the shutdown deadline** — `Worker._emit` retries a failing batch `max_retries + 1` times, and its wait between attempts is `self._stop.wait(...)`, which returns at once because the stop event is set for the whole of `_final_drain`. The exit drain therefore cost up to `max_retries + 1` times one `emit`, and when that passed the join the join expired and the sink was left open (SPEC-027 FR-004). Measured at `71e42dd` with a sink whose `emit` waits 0.5 s and raises, under `shutdown(timeout=1.2)`: 1.21 s, 3 attempts, `close()` called 0 times and `lost 0 item(s)`, though three events were inside the emit; after, 1.01 s, 2 attempts and one close.

The rule: the **first attempt of the exit drain's first batch is always made**, and a deadline of `None` declines nothing. Before each later retry, `now + the batch's longest attempt + _JOIN_MARGIN > deadline` declines it (`Worker._retry_declined`). Since SPEC-090 the exit drain sends its backlog in several batches (*A backlog drains in larger batches, and capacity is not a setting*, `docs/decisions/pipeline/`), so the same question is asked **before a later exit batch's first attempt**, with the longest attempt any exit batch has made, and that batch is declined unattempted when the prediction says it would overrun or when an exit batch has already declined a retry, through the 1.x loop or an engine run that reports `declined`. Once one batch is declined every later one is, while a deadline stands: a deadline of `None` declines nothing, latch or not, and a caller that takes over an owed join can record one after a batch was declined. An engine run is timed whole and counts as one attempt, which overstates it and so declines no later than the true attempts would. An exit batch is one whose first attempt begins after `claim_stop` recorded the deadline. The deadline is the caller's `shutdown()` deadline, recorded by `Worker.claim_stop` in the same decision, under `Worker._lock`, that wins the once-only stop gate, and recorded before any stop event the worker holds is set, so a backoff the stop cuts short wakes to find it. The one exception is a stop claimed on the drain thread itself, which cannot join: the next caller off that thread takes the gate over and records its own deadline in its place, so the deadline is still that of the caller whose join follows (SPEC-068; *An expired shutdown is final, and a join the drain thread owes is handed over once*, `docs/decisions/pipeline/`). A declined retry counts once in `failed_batches` and writes its own `lost` line. A declined batch counts once in `failed_batches`, its events go to `batch_lost`, and the declined batches are announced together in one line, `lost <n> event(s); <k> exit batch(es) declined unattempted; each was predicted to end after the shutdown() deadline`; the exit drain's abandoned batches after its first are tallied the same way into one `<k> more exit batch(es) abandoned` line, written by the exit drain when it ends or by `stop()` when its join expires, before its expiry line, whichever takes the tally first. The expiry line adds the events the drain thread holds, `health().events.in_flight`. No public signature changes and no knob is added: the budget is the caller's `timeout`, and a knob would let a caller recreate the overrun.

**Measured with three exit batches** (SPEC-090, `docs/audits/2026-11-14-probes/core-pipeline/spec090_exit_budget.py`, the sink above behind a gated first batch with 30 submissions queued, `shutdown(timeout=1.2)`). On the build at `be686953`, on 3.12.12 and 3.13.1 alike: 1.07 s, the join completed, 2 attempts, `close()` once, `failed_batches=3`, SPEC-062's retry line for 20 events and one line for the other 40, `2 exit batch(es) declined unattempted`. The same exit drain making every exit batch's first attempt (the probe's `batched` mode, at `75e7bf1e`): 1.20 s, the join expired, 3 attempts, `close()` never run. At interpreter exit with a healthy but slow sink (`p04_exit.py 0.02 10000`) the process now exits inside its budget with `close()` run, and its one declined line counts the events the sink was not handed (8,918 on 3.13.1 and 9,260 on 3.12.12, each adding to 20,000 with what the sink received), where before the join expired with 19,980 events in one call.

**A later exit batch is held to what fits the time left** (SPEC-105). The prediction assumes the next attempt is no longer than the longest seen, and the size target makes no such promise: it is half an interval of the last batch's rate (*A backlog drains in larger batches, and capacity is not a setting*, `docs/decisions/pipeline/`), so after a short delivered exit batch the next can be many times longer than any attempt the prediction has seen. A sink that recovers by exit, its last steady batch failed so its first exit batch is 20 events in 0.04 s, was handed a second of 220 to 250 events, about 0.5 s, which the prediction let start; when that failing steady batch had taken about 0.2 s of the budget to fail, leaving the exit drain the timeout less about 0.2 s, it ran past a 0.45 to 0.65 s budget and left the sink open. So a delivered exit batch whose emit did not straddle a sink change records its rate, `size / elapsed`, in `Worker._exit_rate` (`0.0` when `elapsed` is zero or below, or the emit straddled a change), and after every emitted exit batch that did not straddle one, delivered or failed, `Worker._fit_exit_target` lowers the target to at most `_exit_rate × (deadline − now − _JOIN_MARGIN)`. A failed exit batch still keeps its target, and the bound then lowers what it kept to the time now left, so a sink that delivers, fails quickly a few times and delivers again is not handed a batch sized for a budget that has gone. The bound is applied only when that room is at least `_exit_longest`, which is when the prediction, asked then, would let the next batch start: below that line the batch is declined whatever its size, and a bound applied past the deadline drove the target to zero or below and cut the declined backlog into dozens of batches where the line names one. The bound only ever lowers a target. **A declined exit batch restores the rate rule's target** (SPEC-105's second diff review): the guard is asked when an exit batch returns and the prediction again before the next batch, after its items were taken, so a room just at `_exit_longest` lowered the target, the batch was declined anyway, and the whole declined backlog was cut at the lowered target (980 events in 49 declined batches where the rate rule's target names 8, on a fake clock). So once a rate is recorded, a declined exit batch sets the target back to `min(cap, _exit_rate × flush_interval / 2)`, what the size rule gave the batch that recorded the rate; with no rate recorded it leaves the target as it was, as SPEC-090 has it. This refines SPEC-090's rule that a declined exit batch leaves the target as it was where the bound lowered it. Read from the code, the restore can also set a target the bound did not lower, and each such case changes only how a backlog already declined is cut: an empty exit batch after a rate is recorded zeroes the target and a later decline restores the rate rule's, cutting the declined backlog into fewer batches; a `flush_interval` a caller changed since the rate was recorded, which the restore reads where the size rule read the old one; and a forked child's stale rate (`Worker._reinit_after_fork`'s docstring). None delivers an event differently; after a takeover that replaces the deadline with `None` following a decline, the rest of the backlog is delivered cut at the restored target, as the size rule alone cuts it. A restore is the size rule's own target, cap included. Since the restore the guard decides only the size of the first declined batch, so its failure moves the declined line's batch count by at most one and delivers no event differently; its removal, its weakening to `room > 0` and its boundary each have a test. Measured with `docs/audits/2026-11-14-probes/core-pipeline/spec103_recovered_slow_exit.py 2 2000 <t>`, two runs a cell: at `d9f2f097` the join expired at 0.45, 0.5, 0.6 and 0.65 s on 3.13.1; at `e46c367c` it completed at all four on 3.12.12 and 3.13.1 (0.34 to 0.53 s, the second exit batch 36 to 128 events), and at 2 s and 30 s the drain is what it was (3,982 events handed at 30 s). Its limits: it trusts the recorded rate, so a sink that slows during the exit, a retry predicted from a call that failed at once, and a first exit batch longer than the budget can still overrun (`docs/architecture.md` §12, "The exit drain sizes a batch from a rate it measured earlier…"), and a batch is never fewer than `batch_size` submissions.

**Why this refines "a shutdown shortens a wait; it must never skip work"** rather than breaking it: no attempt in flight is cut short, no sink reads anything new, and a retry or a batch predicted to end after the deadline is not work the process gets, since the join expires, the sink is left open and the process exits or is frozen. The trade is that a retry or a batch whose real duration would have been shorter than the longest attempt seen can be declined though it would have fitted, taken because the alternative is a sink open for the life of the process. A second trade, since SPEC-090: in a process that goes on running after an expired `shutdown()`, the batches the exit drain declined are counted lost, where the drain thread used to deliver them late. Two more, since SPEC-105: at a tight budget the sink is handed fewer events than the size target would give it, and the join completes (at a 0.45 s budget, a second exit batch of 36 to 44 events where the target gave 222 to 236); and when the target's next batch would end inside the last `_JOIN_MARGIN` before the deadline, a batch the earlier rule started and, by luck, delivered whole is trimmed to end by the deadline less the margin and its tail is declined, counted and announced (on a fake clock with a 0.6 s deadline and 74 spans, 20 and 122 events with 6 declined, against 20 and 128 ending at 0.578 s with none). Andrew chose the second on 2026-11-17: the margin is the rule everywhere at exit. The restore carries a third, smaller one: the first declined batch was already taken at the lowered target, so a decline after the guard passed names at most one declined batch more than the rate rule's target would (9 against 8 on the fake clock above), and only when the room at the fit exceeds the guard's line by less than the time from the fit to the next prediction, which can include the next batch's take, a flush marker's answer or a wait for the GIL. A `retarget` between exit batches costs nothing here: the old sink's rate stands until the new sink delivers an exit batch, and it can lower only a target stamped with the old sink generation, which the size rule already reads as zero. During a `shutdown()`'s exit drain no `retarget` can reassign at all, since the shutdown bumps the retirement count in the same section that claims the stop and `retarget` refuses once it has moved, so the generation check on the exit path is defensive.

**Rejected:**

- **Making every exit batch's first attempt** (SPEC-090's first draft). With one exit batch the first-attempt rule cost at most one extra attempt; with many it costs one per batch, and against the slow failing sink above it turned a completed join into an expired one with the sink left open (measured above, the `batched` mode).
- **A closer-grace reserve subtracted from the deadline.** The first design reserved the closer grace so `close()` would fit. It declined retries that would have delivered, and its premise was false: the worker's own sink is closed inline on the calling thread after the join, so the grace bounds only a bystander's wait and a detached close. Replaced by `_JOIN_MARGIN`, which covers only the moments the drain thread needs after its last attempt to be seen to end. The budget reserves nothing for `close()`, whose duration stays invariant 3's recorded exception.
- **Deciding the gate in one critical section and recording the deadline in another.** Measured at one run in 60 under two concurrent `shutdown()` callers, 0.9 s and 1.8 s: the drain declined against 1.8 while the 0.9 s caller did the join, that join expired, the other returned as second caller, and the sink was never closed. Gate and record are one decision, so the deadline the drain declines against is always that of the caller whose join follows.
- **Recording after the lifecycle's `_stop.set()`.** `_state._stop` is the live worker's own event, so setting it wakes a backoff before the deadline is written. The claim moved inside the critical section, before the set.
- **Joining against another caller's deadline.** A second caller waits against its own timeout; making it share the first caller's would let one `shutdown()` return later than it was asked to, breaking invariant 3.
- **A public knob** (a retry budget on `configure()` or `shutdown()`). It would let a caller recreate the overrun the rule removes, and the caller's `timeout` already says how long they will wait.
- **A growth-tied bound** (SPEC-105's draft): hold the next delivered exit batch to `events × (_exit_longest + _JOIN_MARGIN) / _exit_longest`, so each attempt runs at most one margin longer than the longest before it, whatever the budget. At 2 ms an event a healthy exit drain would go 20, 70, 120, 170 and 220 events before reaching the size target's 248, and at an interval of 4 s or more about twenty batches before the 1,000-event cap, each a sink call, where the fit bound leaves an ample budget exactly as it was. It ties growth to the margin, a quantity unrelated to how much time is left, so it changes every healthy exit drain to fix one that runs out of time.
- **No growth at exit** (no exit batch above the target in force at the stop; measured in `docs/architecture.md` §12's "The exit drain sizes a batch from a rate it measured earlier…" at `6b9d50f6`). It slows a healthy exit that starts from a zero target, most for a sink that sends one request a batch (4.36 to 5.03 s against about 0.35 s at a 30 s budget, and about 1,300 to 1,600 events handed by the return against 3,982 at 2 s), and from a measured target it leaves the 1,000 in force at the stop, so it does not close the slowdown there either.
- **A floor of one event on the bound** (SPEC-105's first prototype). Once the deadline had passed it drove the target to one event, so the declined backlog went out in `batch_size`-submission batches and the declined line named 48 batches where it names 1. While a deadline stood its effect could never be observed, since the next batch was declined either way, and after a takeover that replaced the deadline with `None` it cost a trade. The guard on `_exit_longest` makes it unnecessary.
- **`max(0, …)` on the bound**, the floor's unrecorded alternative. Past the deadline it set the zero target the size rule reads as unmeasured, with the same effect on the declined line; the guard keeps the bound positive wherever it applies, so it is unnecessary too.

What stays out: a sink's own retry loop inside one `emit` (the budget measures a whole `emit` as one attempt), `flush()` and the sink swap (a late answer there is still delivery), the orphan path (one attempt by construction), and `Sink.close()` being unbounded. (SPEC-062; arch §12)

## History

### 2026-11-24

- **Refined** by SPEC-105: once an exit batch is delivered, its rate is recorded, and after every
  emitted exit batch that did not straddle a sink change, delivered or failed, the next is held
  to what that rate fits in the time left less `_JOIN_MARGIN`, when the prediction would let it
  start. The fence gains the bound; the entry gains its reasoning, its guard, its measurement and
  its limits, two trades and the `retarget` note; **Rejected** gains the growth-tied bound, no
  growth at exit, the floor and `max(0, …)`. It refines rather than reverses the rule: a batch
  predicted to end after the deadline is still declined, and the bound makes the batch the
  prediction lets start one that fits. Title and file name unchanged. From the second diff
  review: a declined exit batch, once a rate is recorded, restores the rate rule's target the
  bound lowered, refining there SPEC-090's rule that a declined exit batch leaves the target as it
  was, with the one-batch trade it carries.

### 2026-11-16

- **Refined** by SPEC-090 FR-003: "the first attempt of every batch is always made" became "the
  first attempt of the exit drain's first batch is always made", because the exit drain now sends
  its backlog in several batches and making each one's first attempt expired the join. A later
  exit batch is declined unattempted once one is predicted to overrun, counted and announced in
  one line, and the exit drain's abandon lines are aggregated likewise. Title, fence and entry
  rewritten in place; the measured outcome restated with three exit batches. The file keeps its
  name, which names the rule as SPEC-062 settled it. The diff review found the latch declining under a
  deadline of `None` recorded by a takeover, with a line naming a deadline there was not; the
  deadline is now read first, so `None` declines nothing, as the rule always said.

### 2026-11-04

- **Refined** by SPEC-068: the deadline is written in the decision that wins the gate, or once more
  by the caller that takes over a join the drain thread owed. Before, the entry read as though the
  winning decision were the only writer, which a `shutdown()` made inside a sink's `emit` broke —
  that caller won the gate and then raised out of `Thread.join`.

### 2026-11-03

- **Settled** by SPEC-062. Refines *A shutdown shortens a *wait*; it must never skip *work**.
