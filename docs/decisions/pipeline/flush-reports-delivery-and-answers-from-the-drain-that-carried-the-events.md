type: Decision
title: "`flush()` reports whether the drain that carried the events finished, answers from that drain, and counts what its window lost"
description: "a marker that finds nothing pending inherits the outcome of the emit ahead of it in the FIFO. `ok=True` means that drain finished and abandoned no batch while the call was outstanding, not that the sink took every event: a settled run's losses are counted on the result, over a process-wide window from before the sweep to the drain's answer, never folded into its truth. **Narrowed:** an expiring `shutdown()` answers markers pessimistically, so `abandoned` does not imply the drain adjudicated. (SPEC-021, SPEC-036, SPEC-050, SPEC-089)"
---

**`flush()` reports whether the drain that carried the events finished, answers from that drain, and counts what its window lost** — the marker brings the emit's *outcome* back, so `True` means the drain that carried the events finished and abandoned no batch while the call was outstanding, not merely that a drain ran. **Reversed in part by SPEC-089 (2026-11-16)**, where this said `True` meant every event reached the sink: a sink on the verdict contract can settle a run that lost every event (all dropped, all unknown, or one accepted and the rest rejected), and the result stays truthy over it by Andrew's choice. That loss is **counted on the result** instead: `FlushResult` carries `overflow_lost`, `batch_lost`, `sink_lost`, `drain_lost` and `stranded`, with `lost` their total. A marker that finds nothing pending inherits the outcome of the emit that carried what was ahead of it in the FIFO — otherwise a second concurrent flush reports success for events the first one just abandoned. It is not a verdict on every batch ever sent; `health().failed_batches` is the cumulative record. (SPEC-021) **What it reports *over* is three places, not one** (SPEC-036): the queue, the buffers on spans still **open** (since SPEC-091 every open span, not only the calling context's), and the sink's own **client** buffer. Each was a place `flush()` returned success while events sat undelivered — the in-span case zero of two events on the README's own recipe. A span is swept and left open, its boundary events backfilled first (so they carry the baggage as of the flush, not the close) and its buffer detached by swap, never cleared; a swept span then makes `continue_trace()` refuse, or one span carries two trace ids. ~~The bound is the calling context, because `contextvars` cannot enumerate another thread's.~~ **Widened by SPEC-091 (2026-11-17):** the calling context's spans still ship whole, `span.start` backfilled, and then a sweep of the open-span registry hands in what every other thread's and task's open span has buffered past its held `span.start`, inside the same window and under the same `"abandoned"` verdict when it fails (*An open span's events ship on the worker's interval*, this area). A sink that buffers in a driver implements the optional `flush()`, probed by `flush_sink` — which **propagates** where `read_losses` swallows, since a swallowed flush failure is a sink the worker believes. **Narrowed 2026-09-02 (SPEC-050 FR-001):** a `shutdown()` that *expires* answers the outstanding markers pessimistically rather than leaving them to a drain it has just given up on — including one the drain thread is holding, one taken after that sweep, which answers itself, one whose `flush()` enqueued it after the sweep had run, and one that never reached a full queue at all — the last two caught by `flush` itself, before and after its put, consulting the same flag. **Four** producers of `abandoned`, each found by running a probe rather than reading the fix, and three of them by someone re-running the previous fix's own probe. So a `False`/`abandoned` no longer implies the drain adjudicated it. The converse still stands for the verdict: `delivered` starts `False` and is written only by the owning drain, so no added path can produce `ok=True` over an **abandoned batch**. Before SPEC-089 the claim covered every lost event; a settled run's lost events are now counted, not folded into the truth. The alternative was a `flush(timeout=None)` — documented as supported — waiting forever, measured still alive after `shutdown()` returned.

**The window the counts cover** (SPEC-089 FR-002, FR-003). It opens on the calling thread before the sweep, because the sweep's own hand-over can overflow a full queue into `overflow_lost` before any marker exists. It closes where the drain answers the marker, which takes a ledger snapshot after every event ahead of the marker has settled and before it sets the event, so a truthy verdict and a zero count are judged at the same moment and a batch the drain loses after answering is not counted. Both answerers close it, `_drain`'s marker branch and `_final_drain` (invariant 6). Where no drain answers with a snapshot — no worker, a call that returned before queueing a marker, a timeout, a pessimistic answer — the caller reads the end itself, and waits first, idle and within its own deadline, for a drain that is dying, so `drain_lost` and `stranded` are not missed; it never waits on the drain thread itself. That wait ends when the dying drain has debited what it held (`Worker._drain_accounted`), which is before its stderr line, not when its thread ends: a `join` also waited out the line, and a `flush()` whose own thread held a lock the line needs waited on it forever (the second diff review). The drain's snapshot only tries the ledger's registration lock, since a `flush()` or `shutdown()` made inside that lock's section waits on the drain; when another thread holds it the caller reads the end instead. A window a fork crosses is the child's. The five terms are the ones an event handed to the worker can leave by; `in_span_lost` and `orphan_lost` stay in `health()`, since a flush carries no event that can leave through them. The counts are process-wide differences of two `EventLedger` readings and **over-count**: other threads' events ahead of the marker, other threads' submissions that overflow while the call waits, and any orphan log's `sink_lost` in the window. A zero count covers only the terms the ledger counts: a loss a sink takes inside an `emit` that returned (a failed Kafka delivery report, a `MultiSink` child that raised, an `emit`-only sink's own loss) stays in `health().sink`. `Worker.retarget`'s internal flushes ask for no snapshot and never wait, so a swap's timing is unchanged.

**Rejected:** closure (a), `flush()` falsy over any loss in its window — Andrew chose (b), keeping truthiness and adding counts; one `lost` total with no breakdown, which cannot tell a refusing destination from a slow one; the window's difference as an `EventLedger`, whose gauges can fall below zero; a new public type for the five; counting per caller, which would need every event tagged with the flush that carried it. **Do NOT** fold a count into `__bool__`; do NOT add `in_span_lost` or `orphan_lost` to the counts; do NOT have `retarget` snapshot or wait.

## History

### 2026-11-17

- **Refined** by SPEC-091: the bound "the calling context, because `contextvars` cannot enumerate
  another thread's" becomes every registered open span. The caller's own spans ship whole; every
  other open span hands over what it holds past its `span.start`, which stays held. A registry
  sweep that raises, takes nothing because its own thread already holds the sweep hold, or meets a
  hand-in fault reports `"abandoned"`, as a failed own-context sweep does.

### 2026-11-16

- **Reversed in part** by SPEC-089. Asked on 2026-11-15 "Should flush() stay truthy over a counted loss and gain loss counts, or turn falsy?", Andrew answered "Keep truthy, add counts". The entry no longer claims that `True` means every event reached the sink, nor that no path can produce `ok=True` over a lost event: a truthy result means the drain that carried them finished and abandoned no batch in the window, and a settled run's losses are counted on the result. The marker mechanism, the three places a flush reports over, and SPEC-050's narrowing all stand. The title was rewritten; the file name is kept.

### 2026-09-02

- **Narrowed** by SPEC-050 FR-001: a `shutdown()` that expires answers the outstanding markers pessimistically.

### 2026-07-30

- **Settled.**
